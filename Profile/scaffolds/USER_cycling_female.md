### Female Athlete & Menstrual Cycle Periodization

- **Vitality Ceiling (Salutogenesis, Athletic Performance & Cycle Autoregulation):**
  - High-performance cycle autoregulation (McNulty et al., *Sports Med* 2020; Colenso-Semple et al., *Front Sports Act Living* 2023): Menstrual phase has trivial-to-negligible deterministic effects on baseline strength and hypertrophy; athletic programming is driven by individualized symptom tracking, readiness autoregulation, and progressive overload rather than rigid calendar cycle-syncing.
  - Endocrine dynamics & Substrate utilization:
    - *Early-to-mid follicular phase:* Nadir of progesterone with rising estradiol; optimal window for neuromuscular work capacity and glycogen storage.
    - *Mid-to-late luteal phase:* Progesterone elevation shifts basal core temperature upward and increases protein catabolism; mandates proactive peri-workout hydration, electrolyte replenishment, and sufficient essential amino acid intake.
  - Practical phase tracking tiers:
    - *Field surrogate tier (Coaching reality):* Longitudinal symptom diary plus waking biphasic basal body temperature (BBT) shift (sustained 0.2 to 0.5 degrees Celsius rise confirming post-ovulatory luteal progesterone) plus home urinary luteinizing hormone (LH) surge kits.
    - *Research verification tier (Elliott-Sale et al., 2021):* Three-step laboratory confirmation across at least three consecutive cycles (calendar history, daily urinary LH detection, and mid-follicular/mid-luteal serum estradiol and progesterone draws).

- **Clinical Floor (Harm Reduction, Hematology & RED-S Surveillance):**
  - Iron & Ferritin kinetics (Hepcidin regulation):
    - Most recent serum ferritin: `<FERRITIN_VALUE>` ng/mL on `<DATE>` at `<LAB_NAME>`.
    - Diagnostic threshold: Serum ferritin concentration under thirty ng/mL denotes non-anemic iron deficiency in female athletes (Sim et al., *Eur J Appl Physiol* 2019; Peeling et al., *IJSNEM* 2014).
    - Single-laboratory rule: Compare values only against tests from the exact same laboratory platform to avoid inter-assay confounding.
    - Exercise hepcidin kinetics (Peeling et al., 2014; McKay et al., 2020): Strenuous exercise induces interleukin-6 (IL-6) inflammatory signaling, triggering serum hepcidin surges that peak three to six hours post-workout and transiently block ferroportin-mediated intestinal iron absorption.
    - Oral supplementation protocol (Stoffel et al., *Lancet Haematol* 2017): Doses exceeding sixty mg elemental iron induce hepcidin refractory periods lasting 24 hours. Administer oral iron on alternate mornings away from high-intensity training to double fractional intestinal absorption and minimize gastrointestinal side effects.
    - Menses blood loss: <NORMAL / HEAVY MENSTRUAL BLEEDING (HMB)>.
  - Energy Availability & RED-S clinical surveillance (Mountjoy et al., *Br J Sports Med* 2023):
    - Clinical framework: Evaluated via the IOC REDs Clinical Assessment Tool Version 2 (IOC REDs CAT2); monitor Low Energy Availability (LEA) as an adaptable-to-problematic continuum rather than a static mathematical calculation.
    - Multi-system indicators: Screen for functional hypothalamic amenorrhea, suppressed free triiodothyronine (T3), low IGF-1, and blunted resting metabolic rate.
    - Bone mineral density screening: Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) lumbar spine and femoral neck Z-score; athletic Z-score below minus 1.0 indicates low bone mass requiring clinical workup (ACSM / IOC Consensus).
